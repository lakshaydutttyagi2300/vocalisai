## Little and often beats long and rare

20-30 minutes every day is far more effective than 4 hours once a week. Your brain consolidates new skills
between sessions. The *learning streak* on the Home page is there to encourage the habit.

## Type the code yourself

Reading code feels like understanding; typing and running it *is* understanding. Never just copy-paste
examples - type them, change them, break them on purpose and see what happens.

## Struggle a little before looking at the answer

The hints in each exercise appear one at a time for a reason. A few minutes of productive struggle makes the
solution stick. If you do look at the solution, close it and solve the exercise again from memory tomorrow.

## Use spaced repetition

This app schedules reviews of completed lessons after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30... days. Reviewing just before you would
forget is the most efficient way to build long-term memory. Do the revision quiz when the Home page says
something is due.

## Explain in your own words

After a lesson, write a short note (in the lesson's *Revise & next* tab) explaining the main idea as if to a
friend. If you cannot explain it simply, re-read that part.

## Build small things

Projects turn knowledge into skill. After every few lessons, try a project stage - or invent your own tiny
program: a tip calculator, a word counter, a list of your favourite films.

## Errors are progress

Every error message you read and fix is a skill you are building. Professional programmers see errors all day.

## Mix Python and SQL

The two tracks are independent, so you can alternate: SQL when you want quick wins with data, Python when you
want to build things. Later levels and projects combine them.

## When you feel stuck

- Go back one lesson and redo its exercises.
- Read the *Common mistakes* section of the lesson again.
- Search the Knowledge Base.
- Take a break and come back tomorrow - sleep genuinely helps learning.
